        How to think about it

        {{RULE}}

        Before you write a word.

        Small is enough.

        It doesn't have to be the worst thing that ever happened to you. A hard month, a weird Tuesday, a loop you couldn't get out of. If it was heavy for you, it counts.

        You can still be in it.

        No bow on top required. "I'm still figuring this out" is an honest ending, and it's often the one people most need to hear.

        Write to one person.

        Picture someone a few steps behind you. What would you want them to know? Write to them, not to the internet.

        Tell the truth, not everything.

        You decide what goes in. Skip the parts that aren't yours to tell. Leave out last names, workplaces, and anything that could point to someone else.

        Messy is fine.

        Don't polish it into something you'd say in a job interview. Write it the way you'd tell a friend. We edit for length, never for meaning.

        Look after yourself.

        Writing about hard stuff can stir it back up. Take breaks, and stop whenever you need to. If you're in crisis, call or text 988 in the US, or your local emergency number.

        {{TEAR:107:top:#2A2A29:var(--night)}}

        How to write it

        {{RULE}}

        Four parts. Not a formula, just a place to start.

        1. 1

          Where you were

          Set the scene in a sentence or two. When was it? What did a normal day look like back then?

        2. 2

          What it felt like

          Be specific. Specific is what makes a stranger say "same." Instead of "I was sad," say what sad looked like in your kitchen at 7am.

        3. 3

          What you did

          Not what fixed it. What you actually did, even if it was small or didn't work at first.

        4. 4

          Where you are now

          Not where you think you should be. Still in it is fine. Better is fine. Somewhere in between is most people.

        150 to 600 words

        Long enough to feel real, short enough to read on a bus.

        Write like you talk

        If you wouldn't say it out loud, you don't have to write it.

        Use "I"

        Your story, your view. You don't have to speak for anyone else.

        Steal your title

        The best titles are usually a line already in your story.

        Then, what helped

        {{RULE}}

        A practicesomeone couldtry tomorrow.

        Your practice is the part people take with them. Make it something a stranger could do without having to ask you a question.

        Name it like an action

        "Brain dump" beats "journaling more." Start with a verb if you can.

        Make it small

        If it takes more than twenty minutes, cut it down until it doesn't.

        Say how

        When, where, how long. The details are the practice.

        Say what it changed

        Even if it only took the edge off. That's worth knowing.

        Instead ofTry
        Self careTen quiet minutes before I look at my phone.
        Exercise moreA lap around the block when I catch myself in a loop.
        Talk to peopleText one friend one sentence about my day, every night.
